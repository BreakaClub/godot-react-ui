# Styles

This is a strict, typed **Tailwind-like subset**, not Tailwind's CSS compiler or a browser stylesheet engine. `compileStyle` turns a class string and inline style into ordered, interned, frozen layers once per distinct input; native resolves which layers apply (breakpoint, hover, active, focus, disabled) and inherits `color`, `fontSize`, `textAlign`, the text longhands (`fontFamily`, `fontWeight`, `fontStyle`, `lineHeight`, `letterSpacing`, `textTransform`, `whiteSpace`, `wordBreak`, `overflowWrap` and the numeral features; a text decoration propagates to the text below it), `visibility`, `interpolateSize`, `pointerEvents`, `cursor`, `userSelect`, `selectionColor`, `caretColor`, `placeholderColor` and `scrollbarColor`. Unknown classes and variants throw, even if the variant is currently inactive. Matching classes apply left to right; inline styles apply afterwards. `style` accepts an object or nested arrays containing objects, `false`, `null`, and `undefined`. `StyleSheet.create` freezes reusable definitions; it does not register native style IDs.

**Layout and motion utilities.** Margins take `auto`, negative and percentage values; `w-screen`, `h-screen` and `[50vw]` size against the root; `w-fit`, `w-min` and `w-max` size by content (for `width`, `height` and `flex-basis`; Taffy takes no keyword for a minimum or maximum size); grids take `grid-cols-[...]` and `grid-rows-[...]` tracks (`minmax`, `fit-content`, `repeat(auto-fill, ...)`), `col-span-*`, `col-start-*`, `grid-flow-*` and `auto-cols-*`; `content-*`, `justify-items-*`, `place-*`, `order-*`, `aspect-*`, `invisible`, `overflow-clip`, `block`, `flow-root` and `container` work as in Tailwind (`order` changes layout, not paint or hit-test order). `transition`, `transition-colors|opacity|transform|shadow|all|none`, `duration-*`, `delay-*` and `ease-*` use Tailwind's timing and compose across variants (`transition-colors hover:duration-500` keeps the colours and takes 500 ms while hovered); `transition-all` animates every property native can and lets the others jump, quietly. Text colour and border colour animate, and text that inherits a moving colour follows it. Classes with no honest meaning here (`space-*`, `divide-*`, `inline-*`, `float-*`, `sticky`) throw an error that names what to use. Taffy lays the layout lab out within 0.5 px of Chrome's on every edge of 582 boxes at two window sizes, and the transitions are held to Chrome's curves. `scripts/tailwind-coverage.mjs` measures which of Tailwind v3.4.17's classes compile.

**Text utilities.** `font-thin` to `font-black`, `italic`, `font-sans|serif|mono` and `font-['Name']` choose a face from the families registered with `registerFontFace(root, { family, weight, style, font })` (CSS font matching: the nearest weight, an italic face when the family has one, a slant or a thickening made up when it does not; a variable font's `wght` axis draws any weight in its range). Text that names no registered family keeps the theme font, so a tree with none of this costs nothing. `text-sm` and the other sizes carry Tailwind's line heights; `leading-*`, `tracking-*` (fractions of a pixel are exact), `uppercase|lowercase|capitalize`, `underline|overline|line-through` with `decoration-*` and `underline-offset-*`, `truncate`, `text-ellipsis`, `line-clamp-1` to `6`, `whitespace-*`, `break-normal|words|all`, `text-justify` and the numeral classes compile to longhands and render as Chrome renders them, to within a pixel. Unset `white-space` behaves as `pre-wrap`, as React Native's `Text` does. `antialiased` compiles to no style, because the library already draws the lighter look of Chrome's `-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased` (Chrome's default smoothing puts down 12.7 % to 15.2 % more ink than ours); `subpixel-antialiased` throws. The demo registers Open Sans, PT Serif and JetBrains Mono (SIL OFL 1.1; `demo/fonts/`).

**Shadows, rings, outlines, borders by side, gradients.** `shadow-*` (`shadow-sm` to `shadow-2xl`, `shadow-inner`, `shadow-none`, `shadow-<colour>[/<opacity>]`, `shadow-[<css>]`), `ring-*` and `ring-offset-*` (with `ring-inset`), `outline-*`, `border-{t,r,b,l,x,y}-*`, `rounded-{t,r,b,l,tl,tr,br,bl}-*`, `bg-gradient-to-*` with `from-*`, `via-*` and `to-*` (colours, opacities and positions) and the inline `boxShadow`, `backgroundImage`, `border<Side>Width|Color`, `border<Corner>Radius` and `outline*` styles compile as Tailwind v3 does and draw as Chrome draws them: a shadow is a Gaussian of the box's own rounded rectangle, cut out of the box when its background lets the backdrop through (a frosted panel does not show its shadow), a ring is a shadow with a spread, an outline replaces the native focus ring, a border can have a different colour and width on each side with mitred corners, and a gradient is interpolated in sRGB with premultiplied alpha along CSS's own gradient line. Shadows and rings compose across variants (`shadow-sm ring-1 focus:ring-2` keeps the shadow when the ring grows) and `boxShadow` is a native transition property. Against headless Chrome at device pixel ratios 1 and 2, on the Compatibility, Mobile and Forward+ renderers, on Breaka's and the stock engine and in the Web build, a page of any of these differs by under a level on average and by at most a few levels on shadows, rings, outlines and gradients; the arcs of a plain rounded box and the ends of one-sided borders on a curve differ by up to a quarter of the contrast on a pixel or two. A tree with none of these pays nothing for them.

**Transforms.** `transform` and `transformOrigin` take CSS text (Stylo parses and validates it, so units, `calc()` and every syntax rule are the browser's), and Tailwind's `translate-*`, `rotate-*`, `skew-*`, `scale-*`, `origin-*` and `transform` utilities compose across variants as they do in Tailwind 3.4. An element is moved by giving its canvas item the matrix, so layout is untouched and nothing is re-recorded; hit testing, `getRect()` (the bounding box, as `getBoundingClientRect()`), the focus ring, text selection and the text input caret, wheel and touch scrolling, drag and its ghost, accessibility bounds, filter extents and `scrollIntoView` all go through it. Transitions and `starting:` animate the matrix with Stylo's own interpolation (matching lists function by function, others by decomposing the matrices) and agree with Chrome's to a fraction of a pixel. Text and rounded corners are rasterised at the scale the transforms put on them (again once an animation settles). A transformed element is the containing block of its `absolute` and `fixed` descendants, as in CSS. Boxes under a rotation, scale, skew or matrix, square or rounded, with any borders and gradients, are drawn from antialiased triangles with a feather per axis, so a squeezed edge keeps a one-pixel ramp and a rounded card turned or scaled is within a few levels of Chrome's; a box with no transform, or only a translation, is drawn as before and matches a Godot `Panel` pixel for pixel. `starting:` is CSS `@starting-style`: its layer is the style a node starts from, on mount or when it is shown from `display: none`, and the node transitions from it. Not built: `perspective` (refused with a message that says so) and `transform-style: preserve-3d`; `rotateX` and `rotateY` are drawn flattened, as CSS draws them with no perspective (3D was assessed and not built; [LIMITS.md](LIMITS.md)). Rotated and skewed `overflow` clips are not exact ([LIMITS.md](LIMITS.md)).

**Filters and frosted glass.** `filter` applies CSS filter functions to an element and everything painted inside it (`blur`, `brightness`, `contrast`, `grayscale`, `hue-rotate`, `invert`, `opacity`, `saturate`, `sepia`, and one `drop-shadow`); `backdropFilter` applies them to what is already drawn behind the element, inside its rounded border box, which is frosted glass: React siblings, the main layer under a `Portal` scrim, and Godot content behind the root, 2D and 3D. Both are built from engine primitives (a copy of the render target and a mip-reading material for the backdrop, a canvas group for the filter), on the Compatibility renderer (the default, and the Web), and on the Mobile and Forward+ renderers, each measured on its own (Forward+'s back-buffer chain is a different, wider filter than the other two's, and the two RenderingDevice renderers place a level's texels by the whole target, not the copy region); any other rendering method draws nothing and logs one error. `blur(r)` is a Gaussian of standard deviation `r` logical pixels, scaled to the render target; on a hard edge the verifier holds its profile within 0.02 of a true Gaussian's and its width within the larger of 4 % and 0.6 px (the model predicts under 0.01). Separate Tailwind utilities compose across variants, so `blur-sm hover:brightness-110` keeps the blur on hover and `hover:backdrop-blur-lg` changes only material parameters, with no React work, commit, layout or re-record; an inline `filter` replaces the whole list. Both transition (`transition: { properties: ['filter', 'backdropFilter'], ... }`), function by function. An element without a filter, and a tree without any, create no item, material or shader. A frosted panel or filtered element costs roughly 0.5 to 2 ms per frame on an Apple M1 Max at 1120 x 800, nearly flat in size and radius (measured on Compatibility and Mobile; Forward+ cost was not measured), and the first use of each shader compiles it (about 50 ms on Compatibility; 0.8 to 1 s on Mobile with a cold shader cache, 17 to 36 ms with the engine's disk cache). Nothing warms the shaders up automatically, so a loading screen may show a 1 x 1 `backdrop-blur-sm` element (and a 1 x 1 `blur-sm` one for `filter`) to pay that early. Limits: an element can have both `filter` and `backdropFilter` (`backdrop-blur-xl drop-shadow-lg`): its backdrop is drawn beside its group, not in it, so unlike CSS the filter does not apply to the backdrop's result (a drop shadow is cast by the element's own fill, not by the frosted area, and shows through a translucent fill); on Compatibility and Web a group's buffer is the opaque render target's RGB10_A2 back buffer, so alpha inside a filter group has four levels (a half-transparent filtered element is drawn at a third or two thirds); a `filter` inside a `filter`, or a `backdropFilter` inside a `filter`, is reported once with an `ERROR` and not drawn (Godot's canvas groups do not nest); the colour functions of a list are composed into one matrix after the blur, so values that leave 0 to 1 between two functions are not clamped between them as a browser does; a list has at most one `drop-shadow()` and no `blur()` after it, and `backdropFilter` has none; `backdrop-filter: opacity()` blends with the unfiltered backdrop, not with the page; a blur is capped by the render target's mip levels on Compatibility (a 600 x 900 window reaches about 50 px, a 1120 x 800 one 64 px and more; the snapshot's `effects` plan says what was drawn and `clamped` when less), and on the RenderingDevice renderers a target that does not halve exactly (600 x 900, 1366 x 768) makes the last steps up to 4 to 5 % wider at 600 wide; `url()` filters, `em`/`rem` lengths and `position: fixed` descendants of a filtered element (they paint in the root, outside its group) are not supported; an ancestor's clip keeps its own pixels, so content beyond the clip is absent from the blurred picture and fades at the clip edge; a root in a `SubViewport` sees only that viewport's contents; a rotated or unevenly scaled root blurs by its mean scale; a blur near the render target's edge reads the edge texels again where a browser blurs the part beyond the window (let a blurred background bleed past the window instead of ending at its edge); a drag ghost shows a filtered source unfiltered; a scrolled `TextInput`'s own text overflow is not in its filter group's extent; a render target with a transparent background (a `SubViewport` with `transparent_bg`) has not been measured, so frosted panels over one are not claimed to work.

The palette is all 22 Tailwind v3 colours, shades 50–950, plus white, black, and transparent, as sRGB hex values; any of them takes an opacity modifier (`bg-white/20`, `bg-sky-500/30`). A frosted-glass page as a developer would write it (`backdrop-blur-xl backdrop-saturate-150 bg-white/20 border border-white/30`) is the demo's `glass` lab (`demo/src/glass-lab.tsx`). Breakpoints use the surface's logical width, not physical display pixels. [Style types](../src/types.ts), the [native protocol](../native/PROTOCOL.md) and the [motion architecture](MOTION_ARCHITECTURE.md) document the supported surface and boundaries.
